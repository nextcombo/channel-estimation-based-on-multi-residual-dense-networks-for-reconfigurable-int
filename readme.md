# Channel Estimation Based on Multi-Residual Dense Networks for Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface Cascaded Channel Estimation [with Python Code]

Introduction  
To significantly enhance the ultra-high data rates and ubiquitous coverage requirements of the sixth-generation (6G) wireless networks, reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) assisted massive MIMO is envisioned as a significant means to reduce link blocking probability and system energy consumption by improving link quality through complex beamforming [1]-[3]. RIS-assisted MIMO has been explored with near-passive arrays to achieve green and sustainable communication between user equipment (UE) and base station (BS), where wireless signals can be coherently combined and directed towards desired directions through appropriate and dynamic adjustments of amplitude and phase responses [4]-[5]. Each RIS reflective element can independently control the amplitude response and phase shift of the nanosecond scale incident electromagnetic wave, achieving energy concentration. Reshaped electromagnetic waves are formed through reflection, refraction, absorption, and transmission, creating new paths. Based on the characteristics of these passive and low-cost RIS reflective elements, RIS systems require very low energy consumption to improve the electromagnetic environment and increase coverage.  
However, benefiting from improved system performance, RIS systems rely on the assumption of perfect channel state information (CSI). Unfortunately, the above work assumes a perfect CSI without considering its difficulty in acquisition. Firstly, it is challenging to separately estimate the RIS-UE and RIS-BS channels unless the RIS is equipped with an RF chain. Secondly, the cascading channel between BS and UE due to a large number of reflective elements may be highly dimensioned. Currently, assuming that RIS elements are connected to an RF chain allows for channel estimation via a compression-sensing (CS) based approach with acceptable performance. However, given the extremely low deployment, hardware, and communication costs, purely passive RIS reflective elements undoubtedly hold greater appeal.
The paper proposes a traditional least squares method, by assuming the active reflection pattern to reduce the size of the active array and thus lower hardware complexity. Additionally, utilizing the low-rank property of MIMO channels, sparse matrix decomposition can reduce training overhead. Considering the sparse representation of level-connected channels, the CS method is proposed in [6]. Furthermore, as the number of antennas on UE and BS increases, the complexity of channel estimation sharply increases. Leveraging the sparsity of angle domain channels, a CS channel estimation scheme based on CS is proposed in [7]. However, the difference in structural sparsity between different channels will lead to performance loss. In addition, deep learning (DL) is proposed to predict the optimal RIS phase shift matrix [8], but accurate CSI still remains important.
In the field of image denoising, previous convolutional neural network (CNN) structures can construct a pair of training images by adding synthetic noise to noisy images [9]. Considering the similarity between image denoising and channel estimation, a deep residual learning method is proposed to learn the cascaded channels from noisy pilot-based observations [10] and [11]. Moreover, a new architecture called Multi-Residual Dense Network (MRDN) has been proposed and received great attention [12]. Specifically, the proposed architecture uses Residual Dense Network (RDN) as a component.
In this paper, we propose two practical residual neural networks to utilize cascaded channel estimation. Firstly, the Generative Adversarial Network-based Convolutional Blind Denoising (GAN-CBD) and Convolutional Blind Denoising Network (CBDNet) are proposed to obtain precise CSI using offline trained neural networks; secondly, the Multi-Residual Dense Network (MRDN) is proposed to flexibly adapt to online cascaded channel estimation; finally, numerical results confirm that the proposed methods can significantly outperform existing schemes ADMM and CV-DnCNN, and CBDNet.
Conclusion
A cascading channel estimator based on CBDNet, GAN-CBD, and MRDN is proposed for millimeter wave massive MIMO communication systems with assisted by RIS. By leveraging the sparsity of the cascading RIS channels and classical image processing techniques, the channel matrix is treated as a two-dimensional image. The proposed residual dense network structure enhances the flexibility of the entire network, thereby achieving better generalization and fitting capabilities, while the advantages brought by the GAN structure are not significant. Compared to previous methods, a deep learning network based on MRDN is designed for estimating the cascading RIS channels. Simulation results show that under the same complexity level as CBDNet and GAN-CBD, the performance of the proposed MRDN estimator improves with the increase in network structure size.
